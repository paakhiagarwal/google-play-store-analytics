Market reach, category monetization, and rating vs. review-volume comparisons.

Market Reach

Top 5 Categories by Total Installs

ObservationA small set of categories accounts for a disproportionate share of total installs, and most of the top 5 clear the 1M threshold comfortably.

InterpretationInstall volume is concentrated, not evenly spread — a few categories are doing most of the reach-generation work.

RecommendationTreat the sub-1M category in this top 5 as a growth opportunity rather than a laggard — it's still outperforming every excluded/lower-ranked category, just not yet at the same scale as the leaders.

Business Performance

Avg Rating vs. Review Volume by Category (January Updates)

ObservationThe categories that clear both filters show ratings clustered tightly in a high band, while total review counts vary far more widely between them.

InterpretationHigh rating alone doesn't indicate scale — some of these categories are well-loved by a small, engaged base rather than a broad one. Review volume is the better signal of actual reach among this filtered set.

RecommendationPrioritize categories that score well on both axes for continued investment; treat high-rating/low-review-volume categories as candidates for growth marketing rather than product changes, since satisfaction is already proven.

Business Performance

Avg Installs vs. Avg Revenue — Free vs. Paid (Top 3 Categories)

ObservationWithin these top-tier categories, free apps consistently lead on average installs, while paid apps — despite far lower install counts — can still generate comparable or higher average revenue per app.

InterpretationReach and monetization efficiency pull in different directions here — free apps win on distribution, paid apps win on revenue-per-install. Neither model is strictly better; they serve different business goals.

RecommendationFor categories where user lifetime value matters more than raw reach, a paid or freemium-upsell model may outperform a pure free model — worth testing directly against these three categories rather than assuming free is always the better default.

User Engagement & Growth Behavior

How app size relates to rating, and how install momentum builds over time.

App Quality

App Size vs. Rating (Bubble = Installs)

ObservationAmong high-engagement apps in these categories, ratings stay concentrated in a narrow high band regardless of size — larger apps aren't obviously penalized with lower ratings once they've cleared the install and review thresholds.

InterpretationSize alone isn't a strong differentiator for rating once an app has proven install traction — users judge these apps on something other than footprint.

RecommendationDon't treat "make the app smaller" as a rating-improvement strategy by default for these categories; validate against actual user feedback themes before investing engineering effort there.

Growth Momentum

Install Trend by Category (Growth Periods Shaded)

ObservationGrowth isn't steady across the timeline — most categories show a handful of sharp spike months rather than gradual month-over-month increases.

InterpretationInstall growth for these categories appears to be event-driven (app updates, seasonal demand, or external promotion) rather than organic and continuous.

RecommendationCross-reference the shaded spike months against release notes or marketing campaign dates to identify what actually drove each surge — that's more actionable than the trend line alone.

Growth Momentum

Cumulative Installs by Category

ObservationCumulative installs build steadily for most categories, with the highlighted markers concentrated in a few specific months rather than spread evenly across the timeline.

InterpretationA small number of months are disproportionately responsible for each category's total install base — the "steady growth" story is really a few strong months carrying the average.

RecommendationTreat the highlighted months as case studies — understanding what happened there (update, feature launch, seasonality) is more useful for planning than optimizing for "average month" performance.
